Naturalest Sort

Sorting words by vibes, using word embeddings

In programming, "natural sort" means sorting file2 before file10. Cute. But what about sorting ["huge", "small", "tiny", "large"] into tiny, small, large, huge—without telling the computer what any of these words mean?

Words that sit on a conceptual scale (small→big, bad→good, slow→fast) should be sortable by projecting their word2vec embeddings onto a direction that captures that scale. Let's find out.

The approach

We feed ~130 anchor pairs (small→big, worst→best, never→always, ...) into a clustering algorithm. Most of them point in the same few directions. The clusters collapse into just 4 axes baked into word2vec: polarity (bad→good), magnitude (small→big), frequency (rare→common), and sequence (first→last).

To sort a list of words, pick whichever axis best separates them (by dot-product spread) and project. That's the whole algorithm.

Does it work?

Each bar chart shows the projection onto the chosen axis. The label on the right tells you which one it picked.

Magnitude handles size, intensity, and degree. This is the bread and butter.

Polarity is the fattest axis in word2vec—61 of our 130 anchor pairs collapsed into it. It covers good/bad, sentiment, and quality scales.

Sequence picks up beginning-to-end and lifecycle ordering.

Frequency is the weakest axis. It barely holds together.

Almost

These get the gist but fumble a neighbor swap. So close.

Nope

Not everything is a scale. Days of the week are a cycle. Seasons too. No projection onto a line will recover Monday→Sunday.

The 2D projection shows why—days cluster by weekday vs. weekend, not by order.
