    Foreign sounds

    Baybayin has no symbols for C, F, J, Q, V, X, and Z. To use foreign words or names in Baybayin, spell the word the way it sounds in the Tagalog alphabet (abakada) instead.

    • Cat⟶Kat
    • Chart⟶Tsart

    Baybayin cheatsheet

    Baybayin is generally one symbol per syllable. It’s an abugida, you see.

    ᜊᜓbuᜑhaᜌ᜕y

    Only three vowels

    ᜀa
    ᜁi/e
    ᜂu/o

    Fourteen consonants

    ᜊba
    ᜃka
    ᜇda/ra
    ᜄga
    ᜑha
    ᜎla
    ᜋma
    ᜈna
    ᜅnga
    ᜉpa
    ᜐsa
    ᜆta
    ᜏwa
    ᜌya

    The kudlit mark changes the vowel. When above it makes an ‘e/i’ sound. Below it makes an ‘o/u’ sound. A final mark kills the vowel.

    ᜃka
    ᜃᜒki/ke
    ᜃᜓku/ko
    ᜃ᜕k

    Precolonial primer

    Guhit supports two spelling methods: precolonial (no virama) and virama. Before the Spanish introduction, only the starting consonant of each syllable was written and the final consonants were not written.

    virama precolonial
    baybayin ᜊbaᜌ᜔yᜊbaᜌᜒyiᜈ᜔n ᜊbaᜊbaᜌᜒyi
    salamat ᜐsaᜎlaᜋmaᜆ᜔t ᜐsaᜎlaᜋma
    bundok ᜊᜓbuᜈ᜔nᜇᜓduᜃ᜔k ᜊᜓbuᜇᜓdu

    Consonant clusters need to be broken up into extra syllables

    virama precolonial
    plano ᜉ᜔pᜎlaᜈᜓnu ᜉᜒpiᜎlaᜈᜓnu
    pwede ᜉ᜔pᜏᜒwiᜇᜒdi ᜉᜓpuᜏᜒwiᜇᜒdi

    Reading written words were inferred from context, so it’s not good with uncommon or foreign names.

    virama precolonial
    kristo ᜃ᜔kᜇᜒdiᜐ᜔sᜆᜓtu ᜃᜒkiᜇᜒdiᜆᜓtu
