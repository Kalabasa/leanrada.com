    Foreign sounds

    Baybayin has no symbols for C, F, J, Q, V, X, and Z. To use foreign words or names in Baybayin, spell the word the way it sounds in the Tagalog alphabet (abakada) instead. Kung ano ang bigkas, siya rin ang baybay.

    Baybayin cheatsheet

    Baybayin is generally one symbol per syllable. It’s an abugida.

    ᜊᜓbuᜑhaᜌ᜕y

    Here are the vowel syllables

    ᜀa
    ᜁi/e
    ᜂu/o

    And the consonant syllables

    ᜊba
    ᜃka
    ᜇda/ra
    ᜄga
    ᜑha
    ᜎla
    ᜋma
    ᜈna
    ᜅnga
    ᜉpa
    ᜐsa
    ᜆta
    ᜏwa
    ᜌya

    To indicate the vowel for a syllable, a kudlit diacritic is used. When placed above it changes the syllable’s vowel to an E/I sound. Below it becomes an O/U sound. To remove the vowel, a final mark called a virama is used.

    ᜃka
    ᜃᜒki/ke
    ᜃᜓku/ko
    ᜃ᜕k
    ᜃ᜔k

    Precolonial

    GuhitKudlit supports two major spelling modes: precolonial (no virama) and virama. Before the Spanish introduction, only the beginning consonant and vowel of each syllable were written and any final consonant was inferred from context.

    virama precolonial
    baybayin ᜊbaᜌ᜔yᜊbaᜌᜒyiᜈ᜔n ᜊbaᜊbaᜌᜒyi
    salamat ᜐsaᜎlaᜋmaᜆ᜔t ᜐsaᜎlaᜋma
    bundok ᜊᜓbuᜈ᜔nᜇᜓduᜃ᜔k ᜊᜓbuᜇᜓdu

    Consonant clusters could be broken up into extra syllables

    virama precolonial
    plano ᜉ᜔pᜎlaᜈᜓnu ᜉᜒpiᜎlaᜈᜓnu
    pwede ᜉ᜔pᜏᜒwiᜇᜒdi ᜉᜓpuᜏᜒwiᜇᜒdi

    Since words were inferred from context, it’s not great for uncommon or foreign names.

    virama precolonial
    kristo ᜃ᜔kᜇᜒdiᜐ᜔sᜆᜓtu ᜃᜒkiᜇᜒdiᜆᜓtu
